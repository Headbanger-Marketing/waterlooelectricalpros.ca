Before work begins

Keep every label and record where the next person looks.

Consider a rental house where a tenant lives and the owner lives elsewhere. Access may then run through the tenant and decisions through the owner, and neither may see the panel labels or the paperwork the other holds.

Whatever the home, gather the panel label photographs, simple sketches, earlier ESA paperwork and invoices into one folder. Where the owner decides from a distance, ask the contractor for a written report with photographs the owner can keep.

Where should panel labels and drawings be kept after the work?

Keep a copy with the owner and one where the occupant can find it, such as a folder or shared file. Ask the contractor to label new circuits clearly and to hand over any drawings made.

How do I compare quotes when each one describes the work differently?

Ask each contractor for a written scope that names the equipment, the circuits affected and the paperwork handed over. Quotes only compare fairly when they cover the same items, so list any gaps and ask about them.

Who needs to approve the work when the owner does not live in the home?

The owner usually authorizes the work and the occupant allows access, so name both in the request. The owner should confirm their own obligations to the occupant, which this site cannot advise on.

What paperwork from earlier electrical work is worth keeping?

Keep invoices, any ESA notification or acceptance paperwork the contractor provides, product sheets for installed equipment and dated photographs of the panel door. Together they show what was changed and who did it.

If I submit the form, who sets up the visit and any agreement?

A contractor you choose does, not this site. It explains electrical jobs and takes in enquiries for review; sending the form does not arrange a visit, and it neither prices nor carries out the work. A Licensed Electrical Contractor confirms separately who it is and its licence, then what the job includes, what it costs and when.
